How it works

An export quote is a ladder of Incoterms: each term hands the buyer the goods a little further along the journey, and each step simply adds the next cost leg. EXW is the bare ex-works cost at your factory gate. Add inland haulage and terminal handling with export clearance (THC) to reach FOB — goods loaded on board at the origin port. Add main-carriage freight to reach CFR. Add marine insurance to reach CIF. Add destination charges, import duty and last-mile delivery to reach DDP — delivered, duty paid, at the buyer's door.

Insurance follows the standard CIF convention: the premium is charged on 110% of the CFR value, so insurance = rate% × (CFR × 1.10). Your margin is taken on cost and the bank / LC charge is a percentage of value, and both are applied to every Incoterm, so the selling price at each term is cost × (1 + margin%/100) × (1 + bank%/100).

Assumptions & rates
  • Insurance basis: premium = insurance rate % × (CFR × 1.10) — the standard CIF +10% convention.
  • Margin is applied on cost; the bank / LC charge is a percentage of the term value.
  • Selling price at each Incoterm = cost × (1 + margin%/100) × (1 + bank%/100).
  • Cost ladder: EXW = ex-works; FOB = EXW + inland + THC; CFR = FOB + freight; CIF = CFR + insurance; DDP = CIF + destination + duty + delivery.
  • Buyer / seller obligations follow Incoterms 2020; this tool models the cost split only, not risk transfer or named-place variations.
  • All amounts are per shipment, in the currency you select.
Figures are indicative — confirm freight/insurance quotes and the obligations of each Incoterm before you commit.
